## 1. Psychoacoustics, Auditory Perception & Nonlinear Distortion

1. **Moore, B. C. J., & Tan, C.-T. (2003).** Perceived naturalness of spectrally distorted speech and music. _The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America_, 114(1), 408–419.
   - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1121/1.1577552` | PMID: `12880052`
   - **Application:** Symmetric high-pass/low-pass cutoff pairing around ~`1 kHz` geometric mean to preserve perceived tonal balance.

2. **Tan, C.-T., Moore, B. C. J., & Zacharov, N. (2003).** The Effect of Nonlinear Distortion on the Perceived Quality of Music and Speech Signals. _Journal of the Audio Engineering Society_, 51(11), 1012–1031.
   - **Identifiers:** OpenAlex: `W1985745258` (JAES Peer-Reviewed Paper)
   - **Application:** Thresholds for quadratic (`x²`) vs. hard-clipping/crossover distortion; justifies gentle asymmetric saturation (`1%–3.5%` nominal THD) over hard clipping.

3. **Moore, B. C. J., Tan, C.-T., Zacharov, N., & Mattila, V.-V. (2004).** Measuring and Predicting the Perceived Quality of Music and Speech Subjected to Combined Linear and Nonlinear Distortion. _Journal of the Audio Engineering Society_, 52(12), 1228–1244.
   - **Identifiers:** OpenAlex: `W2040086578` (JAES Peer-Reviewed Paper)
   - **Application:** Interaction between linear speaker band-limiting and nonlinear amplifier distortion (post-saturation speaker roll-off masks harsh upper-frequency intermodulation products).

4. **Tan, C.-T., & Moore, B. C. J. (2008).** Perception of nonlinear distortion by hearing-impaired people. _International Journal of Audiology_, 47(5), 246–256.
   - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1080/14992020801895136` | PMID: `18465409`
   - **Application:** Individual differences in distortion sensitivity across listener populations.

5. **Winkler, I., Tervaniemi, M., & Näätänen, R. (1997).** Two separate codes for missing-fundamental pitch in the human auditory cortex. _The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America_, 102(2), 1072–1081.
   - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1121/1.419860` | PMID: `9265755`
   - **Application:** Pre-filtering harmonic excitation so sub-bass musical pitches remain audible on small-cone (`135–360 Hz` HPF) profiles via upper harmonics.

6. **Bendor, D., & Wang, X. (2005).** The neuronal representation of pitch in primate auditory cortex. _Nature_, 436(7054), 1161–1165.
   - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1038/nature03867` | PMID: `16121182`
   - **Application:** Cortical encoding of fundamental pitch ($f_0$) in the absence of spectral energy at $f_0$.

7. **Kirchberger, M., & Russo, F. A. (2016).** Dynamic Range Across Music Genres and the Perception of Dynamic Compression in Hearing-Impaired Listeners. _Trends in Hearing_, 20, 1–16.
   - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1177/2331216516630549` | PMID: `26868955`
   - **Application:** Moderate compression ratios (`1.8:1–4:1`) and mandatory loudness-matched A/B bypass to eliminate `+1 to +2 dB` loudness preference bias.

8. **International Organization for Standardization (2003).** _ISO 226:2003 Acoustics — Normal equal-loudness-level contours_. Geneva: ISO.
   - **Application:** `2–4 kHz` human ear canal sensitivity peak guiding cone-presence EQ restraint.

## 2. Neuroscience of Autobiographical Memory, Nostalgia & Individual Differences

9. **Janata, P., Tomic, S. T., & Rakowski, S. K. (2007).** Characterisation of music-evoked autobiographical memories. _Memory_, 15(8), 845–860.
   - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1080/09658210701734593` | PMID: `17965981`
   - **Application:** Empirical baseline (~30% MEAM incidence on familiar popular excerpts) and bittersweet positive affect profile.

10. **Janata, P. (2009).** The neural architecture of music-evoked autobiographical memories. _Cerebral Cortex_, 19(11), 2579–2594.
    - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1093/cercor/bhp008` | PMID: `19240137`
    - **Application:** Medial prefrontal cortex coupling of musical tonality tracking and autobiographical salience.

11. **Barrett, F. S., Grimm, K. J., Robins, R. W., Wildschut, T., Sedikides, C., & Janata, P. (2010).** Music-evoked nostalgia: Affect, memory, and personality. _Emotion_, 10(3), 390–403.
    - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1037/a0019006` | PMID: `20515227`
    - **Application:** Establishes that nostalgia depends primarily on idiosyncratic person-by-song familiarity and autobiographical context rather than acoustic filtering alone.

12. **Belfi, A. M., Karlan, B., & Tranel, D. (2016; online 2015).** Music evokes vivid autobiographical memories. _Memory_, 24(7), 979–989.
    - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1080/09658211.2015.1061012` | PMID: `26259098`
    - **Application:** Perceptual vividness of music-evoked episodic recall.

13. **Barrett, F. S., & Janata, P. (2016).** Neural responses to nostalgia-evoking music modeled by elements of dynamic musical structure and individual differences in affective traits. _Neuropsychologia_, 91, 234–246.
    - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2016.08.012` | PMID: `27526666`
    - **Application:** Individual differences in trait nostalgia proneness moderate neural responses.

14. **Hennessy, S., Janata, P., Ginsberg, T., & Kaplan, J. (2025).** Music-Evoked Nostalgia Activates Default Mode and Reward Networks Across the Lifespan. _Human Brain Mapping_, 46(4), e70181.
    - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1002/hbm.70181` | PMID: `40083173`
    - **Application:** Lifespan fMRI evidence of Default Mode Network + reward network co-activation during nostalgic listening, and boundaries of BOLD inference.

15. **Martínez-Molina, N., Mas-Herrero, E., Rodríguez-Fornells, A., & Zatorre, R. J. (2016).** Neural correlates of specific musical anhedonia. _Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)_, 113(46), E7337–E7345.
    - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1073/pnas.1611211113` | PMID: `27799544`
    - **Application:** Demonstrates substantial inter-individual variation in auditory-striatal reward coupling.

16. **Martínez-Molina, N., Mas-Herrero, E., Rodríguez-Fornells, A., & Zatorre, R. J. (2019).** White Matter Microstructure Reflects Individual Differences in Music Reward Sensitivity. _The Journal of Neuroscience_, 39(25), 5018–5027.
    - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2020-18.2019` | PMID: `31000588`
    - **Application:** Structural basis for heterogeneous listener emotional responses.

17. **Harper, A. C. (2014).** _Lo-Fi Aesthetics in Popular Music Discourse_. DPhil Thesis, University of Oxford (Oxford University Research Archive).
    - **Identifiers:** DOI: `10.5287/ora-xq67jm1vx`
    - **Application:** Cultural semiotics of surface noise, bandwidth limitation, and cabinet coloration as signifiers of historical mediation.

## 3. Historical Radio Engineering, Electroacoustics & DSP Textbooks

18. **Langford-Smith, F. (Ed.). (1953).** _Radiotron Designer's Handbook_ (4th ed.). Sydney / Harrison, NJ: Wireless Press / Radio Corporation of America (RCA).
    - **Application:** Chapter 9 (Tuned Circuits & 455 kHz IF Selectivity), Chapter 13 (Power Amplifiers — single-ended pentode `50L6GT`/`50C5` harmonic distortion), Chapter 26 (Superheterodyne AM Receivers).

19. **Radio Corporation of America (1959).** _RCA Receiving Tube Manual, Technical Series RC-19_. Harrison, NJ: RCA Electron Tube Division.
    - **Application:** All-American Five tube complements (`12SA7`, `12SK7`, `12SQ7`, `50L6GT`, `35Z5GT` and 7-pin miniature equivalents), typical operating plate voltages, power output (`1.0–1.5 W`), and rated THD (`5%–10%` at full output).

20. **Olson, H. F. (1957).** _Acoustical Engineering_. Princeton, NJ: D. Van Nostrand Company.
    - **Application:** Direct-radiator paper-cone loudspeaker resonance, high-frequency cone breakup modes, and open-back cabinet dipole acoustic short-circuit formulas.

21. **Howard W. Sams & Co. (1955).** _Photofact Folder Set 272-9: Regency Model TR-1_. Indianapolis, IN: Howard W. Sams.
    - **Application:** Verified 262 kHz IF superheterodyne topology, four TI grown-junction NPN germanium transistors, diode detector, 22.5V battery supply, and 2.25-inch miniature speaker coupling.

22. **Federal Communications Commission (FCC).** _Title 47 Code of Federal Regulations (47 CFR) Part 73 — Radio Broadcast Services_ (§73.44 AM emission limitations; §73.317 FM transmission system requirements, 75 µs pre-emphasis).

23. **International Telecommunication Union (ITU-R).** _Recommendation ITU-R BS.450-4: Transmission standards for FM sound broadcasting at VHF_ (50 µs and 75 µs pre-emphasis networks; 15 kHz audio passband; 19 kHz pilot tone).

24. **Orban, R. (1975 / 1979).** _Orban Optimod-FM 8000A Operating Manual_. San Francisco, CA: Orban Associates Inc.
    - **Application:** Historical broadcast pre-emphasis-aware multiband AGC/limiting principles informing gentle broadcast density.

25. **Bristow-Johnson, R. (2005).** _Cookbook formulae for audio EQ biquad filter coefficients_. Audio EQ Cookbook (W3C Web Audio API specification normative filter reference).
    - **Application:** Exact bilinear-transform biquad coefficient equations in `src/dsp/biquad.ts`.
